GREENCHARGE ANALYTICS
BUSINESS INSIGHTS REPORT
============================================================

Project: EV Charging Data Analytics
Dashboard: GreenCharge | Charging Overview
Analysis period: September 2021 – September 2022
Dataset used: Final cleaned EV charging dataset (68,625 records)

1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
------------------------------------------------------------
GreenCharge Analytics examines EV charging sessions to understand
charging demand, usage patterns, charger-speed mix, charging
locations, and charging-company distribution. The analysis combines
Python-based data preparation and exploratory analysis with an
interactive Power BI dashboard.

2. DATASET SNAPSHOT
------------------------------------------------------------
• Charging sessions analyzed: 68,625
• Unique users represented: 2,291
• Charging locations/categories: 14
• Charger IDs represented: 2,098
• Analysis period: September 2021 to September 2022
• Total demand_value: 1,103,517.12 (dataset unit not specified)
• Average demand_value per session: 16.08
• Average duration_minutes: 127.04 minutes (approximately 2.12 hours)

Note: Demand is reported as demand_value because the dataset's unit
has not been independently confirmed. Do not interpret it as kWh
unless the source documentation confirms that unit.

3. CHARGING SESSION AND DEMAND OVERVIEW
------------------------------------------------------------
The final dataset contains 68,625 charging sessions from 2,291
unique users. The average session duration_minutes is about
127 minutes, indicating that charging sessions commonly occupy
charging infrastructure for a meaningful period.

The total demand_value across sessions is 1,103,517.12, with an
average of 16.08 per session. These figures provide baseline KPIs
for comparing locations, charger categories, and time periods.

4. PEAK CHARGING HOURS
------------------------------------------------------------
The busiest hours by number of sessions are:
• 6:00 PM: 6,027 sessions; demand_value 100,426.93
• 5:00 PM: 5,986 sessions; demand_value 94,395.49
• 7:00 PM: 5,017 sessions; demand_value 90,150.04
• 4:00 PM: 4,934 sessions; demand_value 72,021.12
• 8:00 PM: 4,208 sessions; demand_value 79,695.84

Insight:
Charging activity is concentrated in the late afternoon and evening,
with 5 PM–7 PM particularly busy. The 6 PM hour is the strongest
observed hour by both session count and total demand_value.

Business implication:
Operators can review staffing, availability, queue management, and
load planning around the late-afternoon/evening peak. Any operational
change should be validated against local capacity and electricity
constraints.

5. CHARGER SPEED ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------
• SlowCharger: 53,700 sessions (78.25%); demand_value 820,743.32
• FastCharger: 14,925 sessions (21.75%); demand_value 282,773.80

Insight:
Slow chargers account for most recorded sessions, while fast chargers
represent approximately one-fifth of sessions. Fast-charger sessions
have a higher average demand_value (about 18.95) than SlowCharger
sessions (about 15.28); this is a descriptive comparison, not proof
that charger speed causes higher demand.

Business implication:
Review the balance of slow and fast chargers by location and time.
Fast chargers may be useful where users need shorter turnaround,
while slow chargers may suit longer-duration parking. Site-level
utilization and waiting-time data should guide investment decisions.

6. CHARGING COMPANY ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------
• Company B: 42,489 sessions (61.91%); demand_value 684,733.37
• Company A: 26,136 sessions (38.09%); demand_value 418,783.75

Insight:
Company B has the larger share of sessions and demand_value in this
dataset. This indicates higher recorded usage, but does not alone
establish better service quality, profitability, or reliability.

Business implication:
Compare companies further using charger availability, downtime,
charging duration, user satisfaction, and location mix before making
performance or procurement decisions.

7. CHARGING LOCATION ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------
Top locations by session count:
• Public area: 13,404 sessions; demand_value 219,295.02
• Apartment: 12,528 sessions; demand_value 214,112.55
• Resort: 8,293 sessions; demand_value 130,999.17
• Hotel: 8,277 sessions; demand_value 115,492.46
• Company: 7,255 sessions; demand_value 110,743.66
• Public institution: 6,695 sessions; demand_value 107,967.95

Insight:
Public areas and apartments are the two highest-volume categories.
Together they represent 25,932 sessions, approximately 37.8% of all
sessions. Resorts and hotels also show substantial activity.

Business implication:
Prioritize capacity and utilization reviews at public-area and
apartment charging locations. For hotels and resorts, consider
charging access aligned with guest arrival, stay, and departure
patterns. These are planning suggestions and should be checked with
site-level occupancy and capacity data.

8. DEMAND LEVEL ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------
• Low Demand: 23,607 sessions; average demand_value 5.03;
  total demand_value 118,727.80
• Medium Demand: 24,177 sessions; average demand_value 14.46;
  total demand_value 349,623.66
• High Demand: 20,841 sessions; average demand_value 30.48;
  total demand_value 635,165.66

Insight:
High Demand sessions contribute the largest share of total
demand_value (approximately 57.5%), even though they are not the
largest group by session count. Medium Demand has the most sessions.

Business implication:
Monitor high-demand sessions for load planning and demand management.
Use the demand-level distribution to support capacity planning, while
checking the thresholds used to define Low, Medium, and High Demand.

9. MONTHLY CHARGING TRENDS
------------------------------------------------------------
The dashboard shows monthly session counts rising from the early
period, reaching approximately 7.4K sessions in July and August 2022,
before falling to approximately 5.8K in September 2022.

Insight:
Recorded monthly activity generally increased across the displayed
period, with a high point in July–August 2022. September 2022 may
represent a partial month or a reporting boundary; confirm date
coverage before interpreting the decline as a real drop.

Business implication:
Use monthly trends to monitor growth and investigate changes, but
compare complete months and account for data completeness and
seasonality.

10. DURATION AND SESSION CHARACTERISTICS
------------------------------------------------------------
The average duration_minutes in the cleaned dataset is approximately
127.04 minutes. Duration varies substantially by location category:
• Apartment: approximately 163.62 minutes average
• Public area: approximately 113.67 minutes average
• Public parking lot: approximately 42.30 minutes average
• Camping: approximately 208.05 minutes average

Insight:
Charging duration differs across location categories, likely reflecting
different parking/stay contexts. These averages are descriptive and
should not be treated as causal explanations.

Business implication:
Consider location-specific expectations for session duration and
turnover. Public parking may require different utilization measures
than apartments, hotels, or camping sites.

11. DATA QUALITY AND ANALYTICS NOTES
------------------------------------------------------------
The report uses the final cleaned dataset supplied for analysis.
Duplicate removal and outlier handling were completed during the
user's preprocessing workflow; this report does not repeat those
steps or assert counts for removed records.

Before publishing or making operational decisions, retain the cleaning
and validation documentation and confirm the meaning/units of
demand_value, charger speed codes, company mappings, and demand-level
thresholds from the dataset source or project methodology.

12. KEY BUSINESS INSIGHTS (SUMMARY)
------------------------------------------------------------
1) 6 PM is the busiest charging hour (6,027 sessions).
2) 5 PM and 6 PM together account for 12,013 sessions.
3) Public areas lead location categories with 13,404 sessions.
4) Apartments are the second-largest category with 12,528 sessions.
5) Public areas and apartments together contribute about 37.8% of
   all sessions.
6) Slow chargers account for 78.25% of sessions.
7) Fast chargers account for 21.75% of sessions but show a higher
   average demand_value per session in this dataset.
8) Company B represents 61.91% of sessions.
9) High Demand sessions contribute about 57.5% of total demand_value.
10) Monthly sessions peak around July–August 2022 at roughly 7.4K.

13. BUSINESS RECOMMENDATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------
• Review charging capacity and availability during the 4 PM–8 PM
  window, especially around 5 PM and 6 PM.
• Prioritize utilization assessment for public-area and apartment
  charging sites due to their high session volumes.
• Evaluate the slow/fast charger mix using occupancy, queueing,
  turnaround time, and demand—not session counts alone.
• Compare Company A and Company B on reliability, uptime, user
  experience, and site mix before drawing quality conclusions.
• Track High Demand sessions and validate demand thresholds to
  support load and infrastructure planning.
• Compare full months consistently and investigate unusual monthly
  changes before treating them as growth or decline.
• Add future metrics such as charger uptime, wait time, revenue,
  cost, and user satisfaction if those data become available.

14. LIMITATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------
• demand_value units are not confirmed in the supplied dataset.
• The report describes associations and distributions; it does not
  establish causation.
• Company usage share is not a direct measure of company performance.
• Monthly comparisons depend on complete and consistent date coverage.
• Recommendations require local operational and capacity validation.

15. CONCLUSION
------------------------------------------------------------
GreenCharge Analytics turns cleaned EV charging session data into
an analytical view of demand, time patterns, charger-speed usage,
companies, and charging locations. The results highlight evening
peaks, high session volumes at public areas and apartments, and a
large share of sessions on SlowChargers. These findings can help
guide further investigation and infrastructure planning when paired
with operational context.
